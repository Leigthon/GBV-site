Anyone can be a victim

Home can still be unsafe

Partners, relatives and household members are often the people who cause harm.

There is no typical look

Age, faith, job, clothes, or personality do not decide who is believed.

Control is the pattern

Isolation, money, threats and fear can be abuse even without bruises.

You can ask before you are “sure”

Helplines will talk with you while you figure out what feels true.

Types of GBV

Physical / sexual abuse

Hitting, kicking, choking, burning, weapons, restraint, rape, coercion.

Psychological / emotional abuse

Humiliation, insults, threats, manipulation, isolation, control, harassment.

Financial / economic abuse

Restricting money, blocking work, destroying property, creating debt as control.

Recognising GBV

Look for frequent unexplained injuries, fear, anxiety, depression, withdrawal, absenteeism, or sudden personality changes. These signs do not always mean GBV — and GBV may not be visible at all.

If you are supporting someone

Listen. Believe. Ask what they need. Share numbers. Do not confront the abuser for them.
